# Visual storytelling

A text-led video is a sequence of ideas, not a sequence of unrelated visual tricks.

## Narrative arc

Use the structure that best serves the brief:

- **Hook:** create a reason to keep watching.
- **Context:** orient the viewer and establish why the subject matters.
- **Development:** reveal information in digestible, logically connected beats.
- **Payoff:** resolve the setup or make the central insight land.
- **Call to action:** invite a next step when the objective calls for one.

Not every video needs all five stages or a conventional CTA. Duration and message determine the structure.

## Scene purpose

Give every scene one primary job. A scene may establish, contrast, explain, compare, reveal, transform, or conclude. If it has several unrelated jobs, split or simplify it.

Use visual progression to clarify relationships:

- contrast for differences
- sequence for process
- transformation for change
- scale for magnitude or priority
- grouping for relationships
- cause and effect for consequences
- repetition for rhythm or reinforcement

## Copy and pacing

- Keep on-screen copy concise and faithful to the source.
- Reveal complex statements in beats rather than shrinking type.
- Give key ideas enough time to be read.
- Use pauses and stillness as narrative devices.
- Make transitions carry meaning or preserve continuity.

Do not add claims, numbers, testimonials, or product capabilities that are not supported by the brief or source material.
